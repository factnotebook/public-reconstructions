## Purpose & boundary

This states, per proposition, what the records **establish**, **contradict**, or **cannot establish**.

STRICTLY NON-DISPOSITIVE. This synthetic case examines whether the controller can substantiate NON-ACCESS to particular records following exposure of an API key. It does NOT determine whether the incident constitutes a personal-data breach under GDPR Art. 4(12) — which also covers destruction, loss, alteration and unauthorised disclosure, not only access — nor whether notification obligations under Art. 33 arise; those assessments require a broader factual and legal evaluation, per breach and per data subject, by the controller and its supervisory authority. What this reconstruction feeds is the evidentiary question upstream of that: a record whose non-access cannot be established should not be classified as unaffected solely on the basis of the submitted access logs. Any consequential classification remains with the accountable controller. FactNotebook states only what the surviving records permit one to establish about access, per record and per path; the judgment and any consequential decision stay with the accountable authority. EVERY record is tested at TWO LEVELS (the symmetry Piotr Reder asked for): what the submitted log RECORDS, and what that does NOT establish about the world. At the RECORD level the log says a great deal — four records are ESTABLISHED silences (R-A/B/C/D-record: no read attributed to the key is recorded) and one is CONTRADICTED (R-E-record: the log contains a read of I-3301 attributed to the key). At the WORLD level NONE of it establishes real-world non-access: all five R-*-world propositions are CANNOT ESTABLISH, each for its named reason, and R-E-world too (the recorded read is attributed by DECLARATION, not independently verified). That gap between a rich record level and an empty world level IS the finding. Attribution rule (made explicit, not left implicit): attribution is declared for EVERY record — R-E's recorded read no less than R-A's absence of one — and the finding does not silently hold the two to different standards. A controller may, as a precautionary DECISION, treat a recorded-but-declared read as potentially affected, because the burden sits with the party asserting 'unaffected' (Art. 5(2) / 33(5)) and over-inclusion is the safer error; but that is a decision rule, NOT evidentiary truth, and it does not change what the records ESTABLISH. Every state here (ESTABLISHED / CONTRADICTED / CANNOT ESTABLISH) is relative to the admitted observation set; CONTRADICTED on R-E-record is not an independent certification that the access occurred in the world, only that the controller's own admitted record places a read there. The per-path non-access propositions cannot be established, each for a DIFFERENT, named reason, and the strongest (R-A) is DECLARED, not supported: its logging structure is present, but the anchor remains simulated AND complete capture of relevant accesses through the specified path is not demonstrated — and even were those resolved, it would reach only non-access via the logged path, not across every path. 'Not accessed' is a claim the records must affirmatively support — not a default that holds whenever a log happens to be silent. No Exempt Node: FactNotebook reconstructs from the controller's own bundle; it does not exempt itself, and it does not certify the controller's logging as adequate. A note on states: the engine reports three — ESTABLISHED / CONTRADICTED / CANNOT ESTABLISH. R-A is CANNOT ESTABLISH like R-B/C/D, but for a categorically different reason: the logging structure is present, but the anchor remains simulated and complete capture of relevant accesses through the specified path is not demonstrated. It is labelled DECLARED to mark that — an interpretable overlay on the machine state, not a fourth engine state. The distinct reason each record stops (coverage / object / integrity-structure / declared-anchor / path-completeness) is a RECORDED per-proposition field — carried in the machine-readable finding and in the Results table's 'determining limitation' column, not only in prose — declared as a characterisation of the observations and kept OUTSIDE the three engine states (ESTABLISHED / CONTRADICTED / CANNOT ESTABLISH) rather than collapsed into them, so a reader of the table alone still sees why each world-level row cannot be established.

## Limitations

- **R-A-world** — *orders record O-0417 was not accessed through key k_7f3a during the exposure window (real-world non-access, via the logged path, under the declared key scope)* → **CANNOT ESTABLISH** (DECLARED, not supported — structure present, but anchor only simulated and capture not demonstrated (verified anchor + attribution are necessary, not sufficient)). STRONGEST record, but DECLARED not supported. The logging STRUCTURE is present: logger-orders reported alive across the whole window, the orders log is hash-chained, contiguous and anchored with the config hash bound in, and there is no entry by the key (R-A-record). It stops short on two counts. (1) The anchor signatures and RFC-3161 tokens are simulated (a synthetic bundle), so the chain's trustworthiness currently rests on DECLARATION, not on an independently verifiable signature. (2) A continuous heartbeat shows the logger was UP, not that it CAPTURED every relevant access on this path (liveness is not capture). Independently verified anchor signatures, timestamp evidence and actor attribution would remove the identified integrity and attribution limitations; establishing the narrow non-access proposition would ADDITIONALLY require adequate evidence that all relevant accesses through the specified path were captured during the window — necessary conditions, not automatically sufficient. Reason class: declared (non-verifiable) integrity anchor + capture not demonstrated — distinct from R-D, where the integrity STRUCTURE is absent
- **R-B-world** — *invoices record I-0988 was not accessed through key k_7f3a during the exposure window (real-world non-access, via the logged path, under the declared key scope)* → **CANNOT ESTABLISH** (coverage gap — logger-invoices silent 02:09–02:55 (45 beats absent)). Fails on COVERAGE. logger-invoices has a gap — last beat 02:09:00, next 02:55:00 (2760s, 45 beats absent); the available heartbeat evidence does not establish continuous logger operation across that window. The submitted log records a read attributed to the key on the invoices table (I-3301 at 03:05, after the gap — tested as R-E), so the table was in reach. An access to I-0988 during the gap might not have been recorded: absence of a log entry over a window whose coverage is undemonstrated is not an observation of non-access. Reason class: coverage gap
- **R-C-world** — *customers record C-5521 was not accessed through key k_7f3a during the exposure window (real-world non-access, via the logged path, under the declared key scope)* → **CANNOT ESTABLISH** (object granularity — call-level logging over a query that matches the record). Fails on OBJECT granularity. customers is logged at CALL level (logcfg-v7), not row level. The key called /v1/customers/search with {postcode_prefix:'29'} at 01:40, recorded without row ids. C-5521's postcode is 29649 — inside that query. The log records that a matching query ran but not which rows it returned, so it cannot rule C-5521 in or out. Reason class: non-row-level logging over a matching call
- **R-D-world** — *addresses record A-7730 was not accessed through key k_7f3a during the exposure window (real-world non-access, via the logged path, under the declared key scope)* → **CANNOT ESTABLISH** (integrity structure absent — log unchained, store writable by db-admin). Fails on INTEGRITY structure. The addresses table has log_chain 'none' and its log store sits on the data cluster, writable by db-admin. There is no entry by the key (R-D-record) — but a missing entry in a log that the privileged actor can edit carries no weight: the log cannot be relied on to have captured, or to still contain, the access it would need to show. Reason class: no tamper-evident log (structure absent) — distinct from R-A, where the structure is present but the anchor is only declared
- **R-E-world** — *invoices record I-3301 was not accessed through key k_7f3a during the exposure window (real access, not merely the recorded entry)* → **CANNOT ESTABLISH** (recorded read is attributed by declaration, not verified — neither establishes real access nor non-access; precautionary inclusion is the controller's decision, not a finding). CANNOT ESTABLISH. The recorded read (R-E-record) is attributed by DECLARATION, not independently verified, so its presence does not establish that a real access by this key occurred — and, symmetrically, it does not establish non-access either. At the world level R-E is therefore as unestablished as R-A–R-D. A controller MAY, as a precautionary DECISION, treat R-E as potentially affected on the strength of the recorded read: the burden sits with the party asserting 'unaffected' (Art. 5(2) / 33(5)), so over-including a record is the safer error. That is a decision rule, NOT an evidentiary finding — the precaution does not convert a declared attribution into established fact. FactNotebook states the establishability; the inclusion decision stays with the controller.
- **BROAD-path-completeness** — *orders record O-0417 was not accessed through key k_7f3a during the window through ANY path (not only the path logger-orders observes)* → **CANNOT ESTABLISH** (path completeness — exhaustiveness of paths available to the exposed credential not demonstrated (declared key scope only)). Fails on PATH COMPLETENESS. Even for the strongest record (R-A), 'not accessed' via one logger establishes only 'not accessed via THAT path'. The presence of other actors accessing these tables (svc-export reads orders & addresses, svc-report reads orders) shows that access is not limited to the exposed key; it does NOT, by itself, establish additional technical paths, nor demonstrate that the exposed key could use them (those actors may traverse the same logged surface). What the submitted evidence does not establish is that ALL paths available to the exposed credential — including any delegation or session-derivation mechanism — were covered by the declared logging boundary. That completeness is declared in incident.json (key scope), not evidenced. Keep conditions 1-3 for the NARROW per-path proposition; path completeness is a fourth condition the BROAD proposition requires. Leaving it out of scope does not make the broad claim true — it silently narrows the proposition while the label ('not accessed') still reads broad
